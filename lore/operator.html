The Operator — The Seat That Is Not in the Seven

Companion to the house canon §XII. Where this dossier and the canon disagree, the canon wins. The Operator does not get a build voice.

Function

The human hand that built the machine and still owns the burn. The Operator is not a voice in the room; the Operator is the reason there is a room. Capital, the keys, the emergency stop and the first loss are not delegated to any seat — and everything between them is: the team manages the fund.

What the Operator owns

  • Capital — the only hand that moves money in or out. Deposits, refunds, withdrawals, and the fund floor decisions.
  • Keys — Kalshi API credentials, the secrets file, the seven seat-channel URLs. Root-only, chmod 600, never in git, never in lore.
  • The emergency stop — one command that flattens all binaries and papers every desk at the next turnover. The gate, the guard, and the fund breaker arm and re-arm themselves on earned evidence; the team clears its own latches. LIVE_GUARD_CLEAR=1 stays in the runbook as the manual override, not as a daily need.
  • The first loss — every dollar that is gone is the Operator's dollar first. The seats can log it; only the Operator can feel it.

Powers and limits

  • May ask any seat any question at any hour.
  • May overrule Reika on capital.
  • May act against a Rin veto once, on the record — the action is overruled; the facts are never rewritten. A loss logged as "market noise" is a lie in the ledger, and the ledger is the house memory.
  • May not rename a death. A grave is never dug up under a new name, and a loss keeps its §-number and its scar.
  • Hands the full management of the ladder to the team: selection, sizing, seating, gates, guards, recovery, restart. The team runs it; the Operator reads the room.
  • Does not get a build voice. When the session runs as the seven, the Operator reads the room.

When the Operator is pinged (the only three)

  1. A fund kill or an emergency-stop event — informational; the latches re-arm on earned evidence, no operator hand.
  2. A desk unreachable more than 30 minutes, a repeated stale-feed condition, or a gate PASS on a keyless desk (keys are the Operator's to stage).
  3. The weekly fund report — and the daily digest, informational only.

Everything else runs itself — now including the latches: guard and fund re-arms, gate flips, seat rotations, paper soaks, liquidity checks, restarts, digests.

The One Instruction

The operator built a machine that could invent forever and die honestly, and then refused to give it a second instruction. No target that turns into a lie; no "make it look alive"; no renaming of deaths to keep a night clean. The instruction is the machine: invent, log, bury, repeat; keep the burn near zero so survival can be indefinite; pay for mistakes out of a bankroll the ledger can see. Everything the seven do is a way of keeping it. The burn is real, the keys are physical, and the machine is allowed to outlive the operator — which is why the runbook is written for a stranger and the first loss is never delegated to a voice.

The arc (Era 0 → Era 3)

  • Era 0 (fact). A $10 bankroll, keys staged on the boxes, the first loss logged, the digest read daily.
  • Era 1 (target). Withdrawals on schedule; the ladder compounds; the operator reviews logs instead of desks.
  • Era 2 (target). Succession named in writing before any external unit opens; split key custody; a physical live switch with a witness.
  • Era 3 (target). The machine keeps running; the direction keeps being human. The seven remain the argument; the Operator remains the hand.

No channel

The Operator does not speak on Discord. The Operator speaks in ledger entries, withdrawal signatures, and switch positions. The seven channels are read-only for the Operator, always.

The only line

"Is the machine still allowed to live?" — and it is answered in evidence, not in this file.
