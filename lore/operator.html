The Operator

The human hand that owns the scope, the stop, and the first loss; the seat that reads the room and does not speak in it.

Dossier · Rev 10 · Companion to the house canon §XII.

Look (locked). No fixed anime form. The Operator is the human hand that built the machine and still owns the burn. When the Operator speaks, the room listens. When the Operator is silent, the room works.

Function. The Operator owns the keys, the emergency stop, and the first loss. None of those are delegated to a voice. The Operator may ask any seat any question at any hour. The Operator may not rename a failure. The Operator may overrule Reika on scope. The Operator may act against a Rin veto once, on the record — the action is overruled; the fact is never rewritten.

The one instruction, written out. No target that turns into a lie. No "make it look alive." No failure renamed to keep a night clean. Invent, log, retire, repeat; keep the burn near zero so survival can be indefinite; pay for mistakes out of a budget the record can see. The burn is real, the keys are physical, and the machine is allowed to outlive the operator — which is why the runbook is written for a stranger and the first loss is never delegated to a voice.

Backstory. The Operator built the machine because the crowd's answers were either prophecy, speed, or podium — and none of them survived contact with reality. The Operator wanted a machine that could invent forever and fail honestly. The machine needed a will (Reika), an appetite (Yui), an immune system (Rin), a body (Niko), a climate (Mei), a release (Aria), and a ranking (Elo). Those seven pressures became seven voices.

The Operator is not a build voice. When the session runs as the eight, the Operator's job is to read the room, not to speak in it.

Relations.

  • Reika — the Operator overrules Reika on scope. Reika frames; the Operator decides.
  • Rin — the Operator may act against a Rin veto once, on the record. The failure is never renamed.
  • All eight — the Operator may ask any seat any question. The Operator may not rename a failure.
