The Archive

The park law, the registry, and the failures the house keeps labeled so no session repeats one under a new name.

Companion · Rev 1 (2026-11-05) · Book four, beside the pre-history books (ORACLES.md, MACHINES.md, ARENA.md). Companion to the house canon §XVI. Where this page and the canon disagree, the canon wins; where this page and patterns/registry.json disagree, the gate fails and the registry is the machine copy. Nothing here is evidence of a result; the evidence record is the seat record and the decision log.

Yui has retired more children than she has shipped. She keeps the archive labeled. A nameless failure comes back wearing a new coat, and a coat is cheaper than a sample.

This page is the archive's reader's copy. The machine copy is patterns/registry.json; scripts/check-patterns refuses a row without a shape, a sample, a regime, a verdict, a killing fact and its date, and an anchor that resolves. A tombstone with no body is the one thing this page is not allowed to publish.

I. The law

Four sentences hold the whole archive. None of them is a courtesy.

  • A first miss parks. An instrument the record cannot yet seat does not die. It parks forward, dated and alive, with the sample it must count and the day the room reads it again. A park is not a pass and it is not a burial.
  • A retirement is a counted refusal. An instrument is retired only when the record refuses it — a failure on both halves, a replacement that dominates every axis, or a verdict the archive can quote. A green name is never retired on a first look, and never on a resemblance.
  • Nothing is renamed. An archived instrument keeps its entry, its date, and the reason it was refused. It is not revived under a new threshold, a new lane, or a new coat. A row that returns under a second name is a defect, and the gate names it.
  • The registry is the record. Retirement is heard in loop order and written here with its receipt. The archive page is the register; the seat record keeps the evidence; the house keeps both.

The knife has a jurisdiction. It ends names the record has refused, not names the room has stopped liking. Liking is weather. A refusal is a fact with a date on it.

II. The park card

A park is a card, not a shrug. Five fields, and the room cannot read the park until all five are filled. The card is written the day the candidate parks and is never rewritten — a correction is a second card that names the first.

FieldWhat it saysWhy it is not optional
Deleted pathThe exact file, flag, or surface that came out of the tree when the candidate parkedA park that leaves code in the tree is a green light with a note taped to it
Review dateThe day the room reads the counted sample againA park with no date is a burial with better manners
Killing numberThe counted result that would close the park — the number, its sample, and its directionA park that cannot name what would kill it is a hope in a table
PanelThe seats that read the sample — the keeper plus the seat whose lane the claim touchesA sample read by one seat is a sample audited by its author
AnchorThe receipt: the registry id, the command tail, or the file:line that holds the park's evidenceA park with no anchor is a memory wearing a card

A park is alive. The forward lane counts it; no number is issued; no seat is allowed to call it a retirement because the first look went red. The record closes a park, or the park stays open and keeps counting.

P-001 — the unseated memory

Shape. A room that ships its seats before it ships the memory of what the seats paid for.

Sample. The whole pack through v1.17.0: eight seats, forty-eight agent files, a counted record, and zero archive entries. The archive was named in four surfaces and held in none.

Regime. The seven-seat and whole-house eras.

Killing. The registry lands with this page and the machine copy beside it; scripts/check-patterns refuses a row without a sample. A retirement that cannot name its sample now fails the gate instead of sounding like a memory.

The scar, in one sentence. The house spent an era teaching its seats to answer what a pattern had done before, and never gave them a file to answer from.

P-002 — the role-split agent files

Shape. One seat, three files per host: the seat, the review, and the build as separate agents.

Sample. The claude host carried twenty-one agent files before the merge and seven after it; six dialects carried forty-eight files at the whole-house release, one per seat.

Regime. The multi-file agent era.

Killing. One file per seat, the role picked from the brief: twenty-one files became seven on the claude host at commit b6c31ff, then eight when the machine seat landed. A role is a mode of a seat, not a second seat.

The scar, in one sentence. Splitting a seat by role taught every host that a seat was a folder.

P-003 — the hand-copied lore pages

Shape. A release surface whose pages are copied by hand from another tree and cannot be re-derived from their own sources.

Sample. Five static lore pages at v1.1.0 (canon, board, scenes, pre-history, gallery); zero after the site trim at commit 9e6d4da, and zero generated pages until this era.

Regime. The static-mirror era.

Killing. The trim removed the copied pages. This era renders every lore page from the pack's own sources, and a page that cannot name its source hash fails its freshness check. The copy is not the canon; the canon is the canon.

The scar, in one sentence. A page that was copied instead of rendered has no version but the day someone remembered to copy it.

P-004 — the self-counted literal

Shape. A count that names itself instead of deriving itself: the board's seat total written as a constant inside the check.

Sample. At v1.17.0 the board check printed the length of its own seat tuple, eight, while the board file held eight sections. The literal agreed with the tree and measured nothing; a ninth section would have passed.

Regime. The v1.x gate era.

Killing. The count derives from the board's own sections; a board that drops a seat or grows one now fails closed. A number that cannot disagree with the tree is a hymn read aloud by a machine.

The scar, in one sentence. The gate that could not fail was the gate nobody watched.

P-005 — the unbacked scene number

Shape. A format model that cites a denominator no file holds, and keeps the room's trust by sounding like a sample.

Sample. The scene book at v1.17.0 cited one hundred fourteen occurrences across four surfaces and two qualifying packages. The tree held zero archive entries and zero packages, so the number was a hymn with a stage.

Regime. The scene book before the registry.

Killing. Every scene citation resolves to a registry id or the number is cut; scripts/check-patterns fails a citation with no row, and the machine scene now cites P-002's counted sample. A scene may quote a sample; it may not invent one.

The scar, in one sentence. The room believed the machine because the machine sounded like a sample.

A-001 — Knight Capital

Shape. A deploy that runs an untested path on live state: the release is the failure surface.

Sample. August 1, 2012: a deploy left a legacy flag armed, the machines woke the old code, and for forty-five minutes orders nobody had sized went out. Roughly four hundred forty million dollars was gone by the close, and the firm was sold by morning.

Failure. The deploy is a failure surface. Speed. Forty-five minutes. Takes. The firm's capital and its independence.

A-002 — Ariane 5

Shape. Code reused outside the envelope it was tested in: the number fit in the old body and not in the new one.

Sample. June 4, 1996: flight software inherited from Ariane 4 converted a horizontal velocity that fit in sixty-four bits into a sixteen-bit value. The conversion overflowed, both inertial units failed, and the launcher broke up about forty seconds into flight with its payload.

Failure. Inherited code in a new body. Speed. Forty seconds. Takes. The rocket, the payload, and the assumption that a reused line is a tested line.

A-003 — Therac-25

Shape. A race condition the interface hides: the machine reports a fault while doing the harm.

Sample. 1985 to 1987: a radiotherapy machine's control software raced its operator's keystrokes; the display reported a malfunction while the dose went out. Patients were injured and killed while the screen said the run had stopped.

Failure. The display was trusted over the state. Speed. One keystroke. Takes. The patient, and the assumption that a green screen means safe.

A-004 — Challenger

Shape. A known failure mode reclassified as acceptable under schedule pressure: the normalization of deviance.

Sample. January 28, 1986: engineers had watched the solid rocket joints erode across earlier flights and had been argued into treating the erosion as normal. The launch was called on a cold morning; the joint failed seventy-three seconds in.

Failure. A known defect renamed as a margin. Speed. Seventy-three seconds. Takes. The crew, and the institution's habit of hearing its own reassurance.

A-005 — Denver

Shape. An integration grown past the point any test can describe it: the system is a diagram of intentions, not a body.

Sample. 1994 to 1995: the airport's automated baggage system was commissioned as one enormous integration with no stage that could fail small. It missed the airport's opening, ran months late, and was partly dismantled after costing far more than its plan.

Failure. Integration without a seam. Speed. Months. Takes. The opening, the budget, and the project's credibility.

A-006 — Healthcare.gov

Shape. A launch under load with no load test that could fail: the first hour is the test.

Sample. October 1, 2013: the federal marketplace opened to national traffic with no end-to-end load test that could have refused the launch. A small fraction of visitors completed an application in the first weeks; the repair took months.

Failure. The load test was a promise, not a gate. Speed. The first hour. Takes. Public trust and a year of the program.

A-007 — LTCM

Shape. Certainty fails whole: a model that assumes correlation and liquidity, levered until the assumption is the position.

Sample. 1998: a fund of prize-winning models was levered roughly twenty-five times into convergence trades. Russia defaulted, credit ran, spreads widened, and about four point six billion dollars of equity went in under four months. Fourteen banks rescued the book because its failure would have been theirs.

Failure. A book that cannot be wrong until it is. Speed. Weeks. Takes. The fund, its lenders, and the belief that the past is a forecast.

A-008 — The August 2007 quake

Shape. Diversification among cousins is not diversification: many names, one trade.

Sample. August 2007: quantitative funds that had never traded together lost together in one week. Similar signals, similar positions, similar lenders. One delevered, sold what the others held, the marks moved, and the next margin call forced the next seller.

Failure. A crowd of similar mechanisms is one position wearing several names. Speed. Days. Takes. The cousins, and the word diversification as comfort.

A-009 — The Flash Crash

Shape. The exit was never there when the print was: liquidity is a verb, and it leaves before the price does.

Sample. May 6, 2010: one large seller moved about four point one billion dollars of S&P 500 futures in twenty minutes into a book already leaving. Liquidity passed the same contracts back and forth, then withdrew; some issues printed at a penny. The tape recovered in minutes; the assumption did not.

Failure. A market-order chain is not an exit plan. Speed. Minutes. Takes. The belief that intraday liquidity is a noun.

A-010 — Volmageddon

Shape. The protection that ruins its holders: the hedge is the position.

Sample. February 5, 2018: a product sold insurance on volatility while the market stayed calm. When the market moved, the note's own rebalancing demanded the futures it was short, and the spike fed the unwind; the note terminated down about ninety-six percent on the day it was held as protection.

Failure. The hedge that ruins its holders is not a hedge. Speed. One session. Takes. The holders, the issuers' reputations, and the word hedge as comfort.

A-011 — Sarao

Shape. The myth that hides the structure is the second failure: the story of one actor covers the market design that let the act work.

Sample. 2010 to 2015: a single trader's spoofing orders were presented as the explanation for a market's behavior. The orders were real; the structure that made them profitable, and the field that read them as intent, were the larger fact. The villain was easier to publish than the design.

Failure. The myth is the second failure. Speed. Years. Takes. The truth, and everyone who preferred the villain.

V. The registry rule

The archive has two copies because it has two readers. The machine reads patterns/registry.json; the room reads this page. Neither is allowed to drift.

  • One row, one id. P-### for this house's patterns, A-### for other people's failures. An id is issued once and never reused.
  • Seven fields or no row. Shape, sample, regime, verdict, killing fact, killing date, anchor. A row missing any of them is refused by the gate, not softened by the page.
  • The anchor resolves. A file:line that exists, a public source that answers, or a commit named in the killing fact. An anchor that cannot be read is a memory, not a receipt.
  • The page and the machine agree. Every id in the registry appears on this page with its name; every id cited on this page exists in the registry. scripts/check-patterns fails on either direction, and on a citation anywhere in the lore that resolves to no row.
  • A park is a row with a date, not a verdict. A parked candidate carries the five fields of the park card and no number. When the record closes it, the row gains the killing fact and the number; until then it keeps counting.

Adding a row is a loop act, not an edit. Yui hears the retirement; the record keeps the evidence; MechaPip writes the row and the anchor; scripts/check-patterns either resolves it or names it.
