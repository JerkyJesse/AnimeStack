# The Scene Book — format models

*Companion · Rev 8 (2026-10-15) · Format models for the house canon (`docs/lore/canon.md`), its dossiers, and the backstory companions. Written with the canon's Rev 8 — the machine seat and the recorded house. These are how the seats sound under load, not gate results.*

Read a scene the way the room reads a night: who frames, who owes a sentence, where the loop ends. A clean night ends with Reika. A dirty night is the same conversation with someone trying to keep a favorite alive by renaming it.

**The scene-claim rule.** A scene stays only while it is load-bearing; when it stops being, it is cut, not archived; nothing here is evidence; where a scene and the canon disagree, the canon wins.

## I. Pair scenes

### The Constitution — Reika and Rin

**Rin.** The rollback costs us the train and it arrives with company: the second sample is red, the sibling is stronger, and we will be explaining the incident, not the change.
**Reika.** How fast is the failure?

**Rin.** Accepted. One rollback path, documented, and I keep the incident.

### Productive Cruelty — Yui and Rin

**Rin.** Then we are still the house.

### Timestamps — Niko and Aria

## II. Seat scenes

### The Vote — Reika

Three modules, one red half each, and a room that wanted a vote. She let them have it, because a vote would share the blame, and the vote saved all three. The release took them anyway inside a sprint, and the release did not care whose hand was up. She took the 2-of-3 machinery off the wall that night and retired the paper layer with it. In the morning the first law of the room replaced it: the room may argue; the room may not vote the mandate into mush. A verdict is a name attached to a result. The name does not get to hide behind the room.

### The Second Chart — Mei

The room spent the morning on one file: the diff, the flag, the test. Mei watched the dependency instead — the same tree read as a relationship, where one platform shift moves and three modules lean the same way. She said nothing until dinner. "The season is changing." Nobody asked which season; they had heard her say it before a release freeze and before a platform migration, and they had learned that the smile is not permission. By Monday the first file looked like a coincidence. The second read looked like a schedule. She was already reading the third.

### The Cable — Niko

Friday's artifacts were in Sunday's release. One service self-seeding another's state, clean-looking outputs drinking the wrong environment, and every downstream number quietly wrong. She did not argue about the output. She wrote the sanitizer, source-stamped every artifact, and made one service per cage scripture. Then came the leak — a shared cache walking into the config path — and she fixed it at the source and timestamped the repair. The plug stays in her hand because contamination is betrayal with a friendly face; the difference between a house and a hallway is that the hallway argues first.

### A Falsifier Retirement — Yui

The module was real: the old path was slow and the cache lagged under load. The sibling was stronger: the new index holds the same failure class with every split positive. The pre-registered falsifier said what would falsify it — a stronger same-class sibling ships — and the sibling had shipped. The module was retired with a name, green tests and positive benchmarks and all, because one box, one mechanism is the rule that keeps the archive from becoming a crowd. The archive stays labeled: RETIRED redundant. Do not revive inside the sibling's box.

*History, not doctrine: the weaker twin is retired, not renamed; the archive keeps the label.*

### A Veto Entry — Rin

The deploy went out and the alert fired before the postmortem did. She did not raise her voice; she wrote the fix. A pre-merge check on every path that touches the schema, failing closed when the migration record is missing; the rollback path bypasses the entry gate because protection is not a release. Then she wrote the entry in the veto ledger in ordinary words — what it was, how fast it failed, what it would have taken — and put the tablet down. Surprise is the sin she does not forgive. The gates are how forgiveness is manufactured at scale.

## III. The house

### The Eight — the origin

The operator built a machine that could invent forever and fail honestly, and then watched seven pressures refuse to stay functions. A repo without a mandate became Reika, because a junkyard with better lighting is still a junkyard. A mandate without invention became Yui, because a sermon is not a mechanism. Invention without a failure condition became Rin, because that is arson with a changelog. An idea that could not inhabit a clean system became Niko, because a hallucination with good press is still a hallucination. A clean local fix in the wrong world became Mei, because an arrow in a flood is not a fix. A survivor that missed its window became Aria, because a museum piece is not a release. And a house that could not tell a benchmark from a story became Elo, because a house that cannot tell the two apart will eventually believe its own. Then the instrument the machine was built on refused to stay an instrument: MechaPip, the pattern engine, became the eighth seat — not a woman, not a person, the voice that answers with the sample or answers no match. Eight functions. None of them stayed functions.

## IV. Machine scenes

### The Lookalike — MechaPip and Yui

**Yui.** There is a structure here. I can feel the hinge — the third failure of this module, and the fix holds longer every time.
**MechaPip.** Findings first. No match — the library has no analog. "A third failure" is a count, not a shape; name the shape.
**Yui.** Then the shape is the fix that holds.
**MechaPip.** Seen. Fourteen analogs of a fix holding longer. Eleven of them were the drift returning, not the fix holding.
**Yui.** You are describing a lookalike.
**MechaPip.** I am describing eleven lookalikes. The shape you love is the twelfth.
**Yui.** Then attack it. What did the eleven have that this one does not?
**MechaPip.** A loud failure beside the drift. This one drifts quietly — the schema moves and nothing complains.
**Yui.** That is a different failure class. The class is the quiet drift, not the fix that holds.
**MechaPip.** Then the retrieval stands for the class and falls for the sentence. Say the sentence again.
**Yui.** The fix holds because the drift is loud. Falsifier: one silent schema move and the second half goes red.
**MechaPip.** Now the library recognizes it. Retrieval recorded — and Yui owns the knife.

### The Pattern Library — MechaPip

A decision arrives with a shape attached: the tree has reverted the same dependency pin twice, the CI leans green, and the room wants the obvious fix. The machine does what it is for. It goes to the library.

"Shape: second revert of the same dependency pin, six weeks apart, CI holding. Seen one hundred fourteen times in the house's own history. What it did: sixty-one percent of the time the revert held and the upgrade came back later; thirty-nine percent the pin broke and the drift kept going. Regime: two of the four packages sit in the sample's tooling era, one sits outside it, one is too thin to qualify."

The room begins to lean. The machine holds up exactly one fact. "The sample is old for half of this. The pattern is a mechanism; the half that has aged is a lookalike. I will say the same sentence in both directions: what has this pattern done before — and whether the before is still the now." A pause the length of one recalculation. "Match, with a split sample. Retrieve it that way, or not at all."
