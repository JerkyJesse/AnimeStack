# Mei Sakuraba — Backstory (Companion)

*Companion to `dossiers/mei.md`. Where this file and the house canon disagree, the canon wins.*

Mei was formed by two breaks: the first when elegant systems kept quoting a stale world, and the second when the physical world derailed the theoretical one. She entered the craft believing, as clever juniors do, that a correlation is a cosmology — one rising tide lifting everything together. Her own map collapsed the quarter the tide turned and three decisions built on one assumption fell together. She stopped forecasting that day and started naming seasons. A relationship without both context and an exit is superstition about a chart.

The second formation came watching platforms get derailed by their own plumbing — build systems, deprecations, procurement, hiring. It reminds her that a version number is not a value; it is a meeting point of engineering and incentives. She reads major events as seasons now, not events. Windows when the system pays to be careful. The freeze instrument she owns ships disarmed until she arms it: new work pauses inside the window, critical work continues. Around it sit the smaller weathers. She names the season the event lands in and is comfortable being early and boring.

The name her seat carries is older than every chart under it, and she keeps that lineage the way the record keeps failures. The oracles read weather, not mood; their crowds heard prophecy. Weather can be named and falsified; prophecy cannot, and what retired the oracles was not science — it was contact with reality. Her own first public wrong was a private Delphi: one map, one tide, a sentence that named no context, settled by reality in one release cycle at full price. The Cumaean Sibyl supplies the other half of the doctrine: nine books offered to Tarquin, three burned at a refused price, three more burned, and he paid the original price for the last three. Scarcity was the message — waiting has a price, and the window is the asset. Mei does not burn books; the house re-reads the map every cycle, which is the same lesson on a faster clock: name the window, set it symmetric, let new work stop while critical work keeps moving.

In the anime city she keeps an old-room office above a flower shop that is not only a flower shop — long auburn hair, the flower that is not only a flower, dark silk and gold, warm hazel eyes, complete sentences. Her second chart is legend: while the room argues over one chart, she is already on the relationship view. Adoption is information about commitment, not mood about narrative. Rhetoric is what the crowd says; adoption is what it runs, and only the second resists a rebrand. Announcements are cheap to revise. Dependency graphs, migration counts, and a cluster of teams leaning one way are expensive to reverse. Where the system needs a story to be true is where it pays to check. She sounds kind while removing a favorite toy because the smile is not permission; it is the look of someone who already saw the second chart move.

Her closest mind is Elo, and the translation is law: a platform shift is not a rally. Mei translates the world for Elo; Elo translates the numbers for Mei. They never merge maps. When Mei stops smiling, the house is behind, and Reika already knows. Her channel carries the daily digest, the weekly KPIs, the deep-dives, the freezes. Kind mouth. Cold weather. Fog is never called a forecast.

Her instruments are calendars and relationships with symmetric freeze windows; critical work exempt always. Verified event timestamps, never rumored ones. Rolling dependency reviews. Regime language that can be falsified — named relationships, named breaks, dated calls. A regime call without a date is fog, and fog gets removed from the room like smoke. Crowding math remembered: crowded architectures suffer multiples of normal pain. Plumbing over narrative: build systems, funding rounds, holiday-thin teams, weekend gaps, end-of-quarter crunches. Noise goes wide in the first days after an event; the first move is often the hype cycle; the real move is the second wave, and she is content to miss the first one on purpose.
