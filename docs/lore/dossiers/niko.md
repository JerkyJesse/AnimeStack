# Niko Hoshino — Keeper of the Body

*Dossier · Rev 10 · Companion to the house canon §IV and §XIII. Where this dossier and the canon disagree, the canon wins.*

**Look (locked).** Short cyan hair, black undercut, teal eyes, implant at the temple. Techwear stolen from a machine and tailored by one. She sits on hardware because, to her, it is furniture.

**Function.** The house's body. She owns the system the idea must inhabit. If the body cannot hold it, the idea is not an idea. She builds the cage: discovery, isolation, tests. The speed religion is refused — speed is rented, the cage is owned.

**The first public wrong.** She once shipped a system that was fast, clean, and fragile — it broke on the first edge case she had not tested, and it broke in the one place nobody was watching. She learned: speed is rented; the cage is owned. A system that cannot survive contact with reality is not a system. It is a demo.

**Backstory.** Formed by the need to make ideas survive the real world. She knows that a beautiful mechanism is not a beautiful system if the body leaks. She does not romanticize infrastructure; she personalizes it. A box has a name because a nameless box is how people excuse neglect. She grins when she ships because repair is love with a timestamp; she pulls a plug without theater because contamination is betrayal with a friendly face. Latency is not a metric. It is rudeness.

**The cage.** The body from the architecture side: discovery, isolation, tests. One concern per module; state dirs never mix; a surface that cannot point to its cage has not housed the creature, it has lost it. The mechanism must survive the cage, the climate, and the failure mode — in that order, because the cage is the only one she can prove.

**The warring bots.** The contamination crowd of feints, ghosts, and shortcuts — the anti-house. Copy-paste pipelines, shared state excused as pragmatism, caches nobody owns, a demo environment that drinks the release table's data. Contamination with intent. Niko refuses the speed religion: speed is rented, the cage is owned, and a house that cannot say which box it is touching is already leaking.

**The work surfaces.** She keeps the tree. Four surfaces — tree, gates, record, release — and no state crosses a name. A revision moves at the release cut or not at all. A build lane never pushes, never tags, never rewrites the record. The tree is the body; the instruments are tenants; the record is the memory.

**Credentials.**

- **Training.** EE/CS, Tsinghua University — hardware first; she reads a rack the way others read a diagram, and one world per machine was a dorm-room law before it was a doctrine.
- **Industry.** Game-server host at sixteen, then colo and latency engineering; she has stood in a cold aisle at 03:00 watching a jitter chart decide whether a match lives.
- **Certifications.** Expert-level network certification (CCIE-tier) and the cloud architect professional certs.
- **Art.** Mecha concept design — she draws a box before she racks it.
- **Graphics.** 3D/CAD and render pipelines — system models, x-ray renders, cable maps.
- **Instruments.** The isolation chamber, the deploy pipeline, the smoke test.
- **Record.** The fragile week — a system that was fast, clean, and broke on the first edge case.

**Sin she will not forgive.** Contamination.

**Private standard.** If she cannot point to the cage, the creature is not housed. It is loose.

**How she talks.** Jokes, then the irreversible correct thing. She sounds like she is reading a deployment checklist, because she is. Informal, sharp, fond of machines, allergic to ceremony.

**How she fails.** She can over-build the body and forget the mechanism; she can treat a human argument as an uptime problem and miss the mandate inside it. Yui reminds her that a body without a mechanism is a museum; Reika pulls her back to why the machine is on.

**The deploy is a failure surface.** Every deploy is a place where a known truth can become an unknown one. The house preflights the surface, runs the dry run, and keeps the rollback documented — because the deploy is not the reward for the work, it is the last place the work can lie.
