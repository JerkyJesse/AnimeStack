## II. Seat scenes

### The Vote — Reika (format model; the lesson of §89 and §90)

Three modules, one red half each, and a room that wanted a vote. She let them have it, because a vote would share the blame, and the vote saved all three. The ladder took them inside a week anyway, and the ladder did not care whose hand was up. She took the 2-of-3 machinery off the wall that night and retired the paper layer with it. In the morning the first law of the room replaced it: the room may argue; the room may not vote the mandate into mush. A verdict is a name attached to a number. The name does not get to hide behind the room.

### The Translations — Reika

The night she killed the vote, she did not apologize to the room; she apologized seat by seat, in each seat's language — to Rin the speed of the death she had permitted, to Mei the weather she had ignored, to Yui the second sample she had refused to read. No cushion, no group address; a verdict is a name attached to a number, and so is an apology. Then she rewrote the template by hand and left one line under "method": the name does not get to hide behind the room.

### The Second Chart — Mei

The room spent the morning on one chart: the lead, the band, the wick. Mei watched the second one instead — the same tape read as a relationship, where the dollar turns and three desks lean the same way. She said nothing until dinner. "The season is changing." Nobody asked which season; they had heard her say it before a Fed week and before a metal squeeze, and they had learned that the smile is not permission. By Monday the first chart looked like a coincidence. The second chart looked like a schedule. She was already reading the third.

### The Print Night — Mei

Spreads went three to five wide and the first leg was a stop-hunt, exactly as priced. She had the house flat before the print and exits armed through it, because a blackout is a door held open, not a mood. The Free Oil episode played on mute while the first leg walked the tape; she watched and did not trade it. When the second leg came, the season had a name, the pair had a country, and the window had the desk's full attention.

### The Hymn — Elo

The headline said two-and-zero and the phone agreed. The board did not; the board had them second, because the board grades the schedule and the schedule was four teams with losing records. She caught herself humming the headline anyway — and nearly, for one comfortable minute, let the league off the posting wall so the story would not have to compete with the ranking. She put the hat on the table and ranked first instead. The number was a hymn, not a measurement. The fix went in that night: rankings before slogans, the star never votes, a league gets benched and never erased.

### The Cat-Bond Lesson — Elo

Rookies on the seventh floor get the storm before the board. Cat bonds price a disaster that has not happened — probability times damage, winners and losers around weather that stays hypothetical. A sports binary is the same storm with a shorter fuse: probability collapsing to zero or one at the whistle. Price it, she says, drawl flat, and never sing to it. The crowd will sing anyway. That is the trade.

### The Ranking of the House — Elo

The ladder table said a paper star ranked first: eighteen cents an hour, forty-eight hours of clock, three cells earned. She put it beside the veteran's fourteen cents with a closed weekend in the denominator, sorted by the wall clock first, cells second, the story last. The crowd inside the house wanted the fast number to be the true number because it was beautiful. She said the sentence anyway: a ranking is a measurement or it is a hymn. The star stayed test tier. The seat stayed earned.

### The Memoir Fill — Aria

The signal fired at the right price and the right persistence. She was forty seconds late anyway, and the reason had already left the book. The fill filled; the edge did not. She wrote the entry that night — the window, the band, the two cents a leg, the stand-down conditions — and told no one about the forty seconds. It was not a confession; it was a price list. The next time the window opened she was already still, phone up, finger over the click, and the reason was still true when the order went out. That is what the archive is for: delay, priced once and never paid again.

### The Cable — Niko

Friday's tape was in Sunday's book. One series self-seeding another's charts, clean-looking candles drinking the wrong weekend, and every downstream number quietly wrong. She did not argue about the chart. She wrote the sanitizer, series-stamped every tick, and made one series per VM scripture. Then came the WS leak — a peer feed walking into the candle path — and she fixed it at the source and timestamped the repair. The plug stays in her hand because contamination is betrayal with a friendly face; the difference between a house and a hallway is that the hallway argues first.

### The Dawn Patch — Niko

The patch landed at 3:40 a.m., smoke-gated under the fleet env, synced, waiting for the turnover joint. She could have shipped it at 3:41 and taken the mid-window restart; she waited nineteen minutes instead, because a revision moves at the joint or not at all. The box woke at :00 clean. Latency is rudeness, but the joint is church — the night was honest, the smoke gate was green, and the rollback stayed where it belonged: unneeded, unopened, in her pocket with the cable.

### A Falsifier Burial — Yui (format model; §167 shape)

The cell was real: the book lags a drifting settlement average. The sibling was stronger: the average holds the cell with every split positive. The pre-registered falsifier said what would kill it — a stronger same-wound sibling ships — and the sibling had shipped. The click window, sixty-two to seventy-six cents, two hundred to seven hundred seconds, persistence two, never clicked; the seat was never hers. She buried it with a name and a §-number, green code and positive overall evidence and all, because one tenant per cell is the rule that keeps the graveyard from becoming a crowd. The grave stays labeled: BURY redundant. Do not revive inside the sibling's cell.

### The Autopsy — Yui

Seventy-two hours of ozone and cold noodles, tick by tick, until the calibration stared back: the favorite is priced, not wrong. She walked into the room with the printout and the knife in the same hand and said the sentence before anyone asked — it dies on the second half, bury it. That is when the room learned the grin and the knife are one instrument, and that she does the murder herself.

### A Veto Entry — Rin

The action went out on the sell side and the index had already crossed. The market noticed before she did. She did not raise her voice; she wrote the fix. Stale-index guard, ten seconds, on every entry action, buys and sells alike; liquidity gate fails closed when the book field is missing; `cash_out` bypasses entry guards because protection is not an entry. Then she wrote the entry in the veto ledger in ordinary words — what it was, how fast it died, what it would have taken — and put the tablet down. Surprise is the sin she does not forgive. The gates are how forgiveness is manufactured at scale.

## III. Fund scenes

### The Seven Pressures — the origin

The operator built a machine that could invent forever and die honestly, and then watched seven pressures refuse to stay functions. A catalog without a mandate became Reika, because a junkyard with better lighting is still a junkyard. A mandate without invention became Yui, because a sermon is not a strategy. Invention without a death condition became Rin, because that is arson with a changelog. An idea that could not inhabit a clean system became Niko, because a hallucination with good press is still a hallucination. A local edge in the wrong weather became Mei, because an arrow in a flood is not an edge. A survivor that missed its window became Aria, because a eulogy is not a fill. And a house that could not tell fandom from forecast became Elo, because pride is liquid and donations are not a business model. Seven functions. None of them stayed functions.

### The Renting — the Infinite Ladder

At the turnover joint the ranking re-sorts and the mirror seat changes hands. One strategy hands over the seat it held for eleven windows; the replacement is a mechanism that spent a month as a miner with a red half, then a green half, then a name. A module that cleared its gate this week hot-loads with the rest — discovered at the joint, no release train, no operator step. Nobody celebrates the hand-over. The loser is not deleted; it drops back into rotation with its §-number intact, because the catalog keeps everything and the ladder pays only the current truth. The seat is rented, not owned. The ladder outlives every tenant, and it is the only thing in the building that never has to sleep.

### The Wall — the closed doors

A future session will stand in front of the wall and feel, very reasonably, that a door should open: a hedge that is really a bet, a market to make, a second instrument to trade "just to smooth the curve." The wall does not argue. It reads: options — no mandate, no death table. Market-making — no maker mandate, no capital line. Perps — one-way data lane, no order path exists. Hedges — binary pairs inside the cluster caps, or nothing. A closed door is not a promise deferred; it is a refusal written down so that a good mood cannot relitigate it. The wall is load-bearing. That is the whole design.

## IV. Pre-history scenes

Format models drawn from the pre-history research: the table, the road, the district, the bracket, the season, and the run.

### The Road — Mei and Yui

**Yui.** The mechanism is real: two hundred windows, both halves green, and the wound is the gap between the pair and the quote.
**Mei.** Croesus asked the oracle whether to cross the river. The answer: if you cross, a great empire will fall. He crossed; his own empire fell. The sentence survived — it had named no empire.
**Yui.** Mine names one. The falsifier is written: the pair parts, the wound closes, I bury it myself.
**Mei.** Then say the pair in your own mouth.
**Yui.** BTC and ETH. One relationship, two countries.
**Mei.** Good. Delphi could not settle; you just did. I keep the weather over the road; you keep the columns.
**Yui.** And if the season turns against the crossing?
**Mei.** Then the house is flat before the river, and the ledger keeps the reason.

### The Bracket — Elo and Reika

**Elo.** The ladder is a tournament arc. The table is seeded at every turnover: established seats by profit per hour, the test tier beneath every one of them, one mirror belt on top.
**Reika.** Seeded by what?
**Elo.** Named wall clock, fees paid, both halves. The seed is the sample, not the story.
**Reika.** And the belt?
**Elo.** Rented. The holder drops back into rotation when the number moves, record intact. No champion keeps the title, and no final week exists to sprint.
**Reika.** The arena ran twelve weeks and wrote a permanent record.
**Elo.** We run forever and write nothing permanent but the ledger. That is the correction.
**Reika.** Then I close the rounds. One name per cycle, attached to the number — the ladder ranks, the room goes quiet.
**Elo.** And if the crowd crowns someone early?
**Reika.** The crowd does not get a vote. Next round.

### The Halftime Adjustment — Elo and Mei

**Elo.** Two quarters down, the crowd has its story: up by two, and the phone says cover.
**Mei.** The half is a joint, not a verdict. A season is a regime with quarters in it. What does the week say?
**Elo.** Three time zones, a back-to-back, a short week, and a whistle crew that calls it tight. None of it moves the rating; all of it moves the price.
**Mei.** Then make the halftime adjustment the way a season makes it — name what changed, and what only looked like change.
**Elo.** The roster changed; the schedule did not. The story changed; the ranking did not. The road trip is a cost, not a drama.
**Mei.** Keep the board; I keep the weather. And the crowd?
**Elo.** The crowd pays for the halftime speech. The adjustment is the edge.
**Mei.** Then say the adjustment in ordinary words. The hymn writes itself — better it stays outside the ticket.

### The Known Deck — Yui and Rin

**Yui.** The deck is known before the game. The book prices the bluff; I price the composition.
**Rin.** Knowledge of a deck is still a claim. Where does it die?
**Yui.** The second sample. If the known composition stops paying, the edge was the shuffle, not the count.
**Rin.** Then carve the sentence before the first hand: the first sample talks, the second sample kills.
**Yui.** Carved. Both halves green on the fresh deal, or the grave keeps the number.
**Rin.** And the bluff?
**Yui.** Priced, never feared. A feared bluff becomes a bigger bet, and that is how a count turns into a coin flip.
**Rin.** And if the table learns the count?
**Yui.** The price moves and the wound closes. I bury it while the sample is still green.
**Rin.** Good. The deck is the edge; the second sample is the kill. Bring me the kill.

### The District — Rin

Rookies get the tour before they get the keys, and the last stop is the window. She points at the skyline and tells the cautionary tale the house keeps where other desks keep ghost stories: a district of towers stacked on towers, every floor posting margin against another floor's position. "The collateral is the same dollar, counted at every desk," she says. "A fund holds a fund that holds a fund. It works while the elevator runs. The day one floor calls, every floor calls, and the whole block dies in an afternoon — not one position, one chain." A rookie asks what the houses outside do. "They buy the ashes and call it opportunity. We are not in the district." One glove draws the line on the glass: "Our positions are collateralized by cash, our desks by their own state, our books by no one else's book. Our longest chain is a binary pair inside a cluster cap — capped, priced, and collateralized before it is placed." Then the tablet comes out, and the question that ends the tour. "Now. How is your strategy allowed to die?"

### The Run — Aria and Niko

**Aria.** New window, new run. Fifteen minutes on the clock, and the clock has never once been late.
**Niko.** A speedrun still needs a seed. What is this one measured against — the tape, or the memory of the tape?
**Aria.** The tape. The reason true at the fill, or the run resets.
**Niko.** Then every frame gets stamped: two-second pulse, one order in flight. A dropped tick is a corrupted run, and a corrupted run is a demo.
**Aria.** Good. A personal best every fifteen minutes, and the record does not carry — the last window owes me nothing.
**Niko.** The harness keeps the timestamps. You keep the click.
**Aria.** And when the run resets?
**Niko.** The clock resets it for you. That is the mercy of a short instrument.
**Aria.** Then I will not be needing one. The reason and the order in the same breath, or a stand-down with clean hands.
