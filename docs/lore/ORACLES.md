# The Oracles — the first question

*Pre-history · Rev 1 (2026-11-05) · Book one of three, beside `MACHINES.md` and `ARENA.md`. Companion to the house canon §XV. Where this file and the canon disagree, the canon wins. Nothing here is evidence; the evidence record is the seat record and the decision log.*

Four surfaces carry the name Oracle in the older houses — desks, terminals, dashboards, and a hundred products that promise foresight. Before the first box, before the first test, the name belonged to people who asked the world a question and read the answer in a bone, a line, a bird, or a trance. The house keeps that history for one reason: **the name is a warning, not a worship.**

The old oracles were never wrong and never right. An answer that cannot be settled is not an answer. That sentence is the whole book, and every page after this one is the same sentence wearing a different century.

---

## I. The weather of the old oracles

The old oracles were not fortune tellers. They were a climate service wearing prophecy's robe, and the robe is what ended them. Read the record the way a release engineer reads a postmortem: what did the priestess actually claim, and what could ever have closed the book on it? The record is one sentence long — an answer that cannot be settled is not an answer — and it is kept in four names.

**Dodona** is the oldest and the quietest. Oaks, doves, rustling leaves — all of it read as weather. No vision, no riddle: a condition of the world, described. That is a climate call in the only language that existed for it. A rustle can be checked against a harvest; a season can be named. The trouble began when the crowd insisted on hearing fate in it. The priestesses read the sky; the queue outside heard a promise about its own life. That gap is the first map collapse in the record: one instrument, two claims, and only one of them could ever close.

**Delphi** refined the product. The Pythia spoke from a trance in sentences built not to settle. Croesus asked whether to cross the river; the answer came back that if he crossed, a great empire would fall. He crossed. An empire fell — his own. The sentence survived him, and survives today, because it named no empire, no river, and no date. Had he stayed home, it would have survived that too. An answer that pays in every outcome is not an answer; it is fog with a temple, and the temple charged for it. The modern form is easy to recognize: the roadmap that names no date, the launch that promises a "step change", the demo that is declared a success before anyone writes down what failure would have looked like. The grammar is the same. Only the marble changed.

**Rome** industrialized the fog. Augurs read birds in flight and chickens at their feed — the story that the chickens would not feed delayed a battle is the ancestor of every metric that is consulted only when it agrees — and haruspices read the livers of sacrificed animals for the shape of the day. The Babylonians had already baked clay model livers on which the art was taught: a training set for an untestable claim, the ancestor of every model that keeps quoting a stale world with perfect grammar. A model trained on a liver says nothing about the liver, and nothing in the practice could be wrong, because nothing in it could close. Modern dress: a benchmark whose harness is the only thing that ever changed; a dashboard whose numbers are reconstructed after the meeting; a regression suite that passes because it no longer asserts anything.

Then the **Cumaean Sibyl** wrote the price of waiting into the record. Nine books of prophecy offered to King Tarquin; he refused the price; she burned three and held it; he refused again; she burned three more; he bought the last three at the original price — three books for what nine had cost. Two lessons, and the second is the one that pays: waiting has a price, and the window is the asset. She kept her price while her own inventory burned, which is discipline of a kind; the house keeps a clock where the Sibyl kept a fire. The modern form is the release window, the freeze, the incident clock: name the window, set it symmetric, and let the repair keep acting while the new feature stops.

The lesser arts sort the fog into families. The **sortes Virgilianae** opened a sacred book and read the first line as the answer — selection by random line, the exact opposite of a sample, and therefore an answer with no denominator to grade it. **Nostradamus** wrote his quatrains deliberately obscure: never wrong because never settled, the original of every forecast that refuses to be falsified. **Astrology** called the planets weather — the oldest climate doctrine and the oldest map collapse, one series dressed as a cosmology, a chart with no second country and no exit. Each family is still alive somewhere: the roadmap read as a promise, the benchmark read as a product, the demo read as a forecast. Each is refused at the door.

What made the whole class obsolete was not science. It was **settlement**. A claim that resolves at a time, in a place, against a real system is the one thing no oracle above ever offered, and it is the only thing the house is allowed to want.

## II. The first test suite

The oracle bone is the first artifact in this history the house can love without flinching. Shang diviners carved the question into a scapula or a turtle shell — a campaign, a harvest, a birth, a hunt — applied heat until the bone cracked, and read the crack. Then they archived it: the question, the crack, and the court's record of what followed. That archive is the hinge of the whole book: the first known record of a question formed **before** the answer existed, put to a world that had not yet answered.

Read the sequence the way the house reads a release. The question is written first — inscribed, not improvised. The world is allowed to answer once; the crack runs one way or the other, and the diviner's reading is asserted where the court can see it. Then the result is filed beside the question. A question with its answer attached is a record; an answer without a question is a rumor. The Shang kept records, which is why the question survives three thousand years after the kingdom that asked it.

That is the first test suite, and the house's pipeline is on the same bone:

- **The question is the inscription.** The failure the change removes, stated before the change exists. A change that cannot state its question is a demonstration looking for a caption.
- **The falsifier is carved before the heat.** One ordinary sentence, pre-registered where it cannot be edited after the run. The modern form is the failing test written first, the assertion named in the issue, the claim card's falsifier field.
- **The crack is the run.** One run, not a hundred; a hundred runs of the same question are one run with better lighting.
- **The archive is the record.** The question, the run, and the reading, filed together. The result that is not filed beside its question is the second rumor.

Then the **I Ching** takes the step the whole research wing stands on. Sixty-four hexagrams, six lines each, each line yin or yang — two marks, six positions, every answer the system admits. Leibniz set the hexagrams against binary numbers in the seventeenth century and found the correspondence exact. The oldest book of answers still in continuous use is, structurally, a six-bit encoding: the world reduced not to a sentence but to a question that resolves to one of two marks. It did not survive three thousand years because its answers were true. It survived because its grammar was finite — two values, six slots, a stated rule for reading the line. That is an answer sheet a book can grade. That is the first binary.

The modern form is boring, which is why it works. A test is a finite grammar: setup, action, expected, actual. A release gate is a finite grammar: build, run, compare, hold or pass. A claim card is a finite grammar: failure, mechanism, sample, control, falsifier, disposition. The systems that survive are not the ones with the best answers. They are the ones whose answers can be graded by someone who was not in the room.

The **sortes** are the counter-example, and the contrast is the whole lesson. Open a sacred book, read the first line touched, call it the answer — selection by random line, no denominator anywhere. No inscribed question, no stated resolution, no way for the answer to be wrong, and therefore no way for it to pay. A question must settle to a binary result — shipped or not, measured or not, green or red — on a stated system, at a stated time, against a stated rule, or it is a riddle. Riddles have poets and temples. They do not have records.

That lands the doctrine in one sentence: **a question that cannot settle to zero or one cannot pay.** State the question before the system answers it. Write the falsifier before the victory lap. The docstring contract — every change names the failure it removes and the exact sentence that would falsify it, inside the change, before the first test is believed — is the oracle bone's descendant: carved first, settled once, filed beside the answer.

## III. What the era left in this pack

Three instruments and one scar.

- **The settled claim.** `docs/lore/canon.md` §XXII: the claim, one sentence; the docstring contract; the sample and the control; the measured bar; the scar. A claim that cannot name the failure it removes is a collage, and a claim that cannot settle to a test is a riddle wearing a chart.
- **The claim card.** The standard page beside the canon carries the ten fields — claim, failure, mechanism, sample, control, halves, falsifier, carved date, receipt, disposition. The card is the bone: the question and its answer filed together, so a reader who was not in the room can grade the run.
- **The first test.** Every change lands with its failing test in the same commit. The test is the crack: it runs once, it is filed, and it does not care how the room felt about the change.
- **The scar.** P-005 in the archive: a scene book that cited a denominator no file held. The number sounded like a sample, and the room believed the machine because the machine sounded like a sample. The fix is written into the registry: every citation resolves to a row, or the number is cut.

The oracles read climate; their crowds heard prophecy. The house keeps the climate half: a claim can be named, dated, and falsified. Prophecy cannot. The name is a warning; the settlement is the oracle.
