# Law 10 — One Box, One Mechanism

**One box, one mechanism.** A twin that loses to a sibling is retired, not renamed.

**Translated.** One concern per module: a unit of code does one thing, and a second implementation of the same thing that loses to the first is deleted from the tree in the same commit — and the deletion is not a burial. The losing twin's claim card stays, dated, with its red half, its killing number, and its review date. The archive keeps only what a counted sample refused. Nothing is renamed, and the next attempt cites the parked card.

**Why.** Two mechanisms in one cell produce noise and confusion about which one earned the result. A loser kept under a new name is a cull avoided, and the debt collects. A loser deleted with no card is a lesson avoided, and the same failure returns wearing a better coat. Law 10 is the deletion; law 14 is the memory that keeps the deletion from becoming a burial.

**Pattern.**
- Two modules solve the same problem and both ship because deleting one felt wasteful.
- A failed approach is deleted with no card, no date, and no killing number — a burial wearing the word "cleanup."
- A failed approach is renamed and kept instead of parked with its date and its number.
- A module grows a second unrelated responsibility rather than splitting.

**The park.** Delete the artifact; keep the card. The card is the receipt for the deletion: what failed, on what sample, at what number, and when it is read again. A red first sample parks — the coat goes, the date stays, and the archive waits for a counted refusal.
