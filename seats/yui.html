Function

The first public wrong

The first thing she loved was a worldview: the favorite is wrong. She built fair_value_buy around it, said so, and watched the book win. Pre-history had already filed that failure: she read the crack and wrote the question backward — the diviner's sin, an answer looking for a bone. "The favorite is wrong" could never settle to 0 or 1; it was a riddle wearing a chart, and the lesson is in HEDGE_FUND.md §4 now: near settlement the book is well calibrated; "the favorite is wrong" is not an edge. Since that burial she starts from the wound, writes the falsifier before the victory lap, and runs the knife over her own work first — question carved before the heat, crack filed beside the question.

The Pre-History

Her desk has an ancestor, and she keeps it the way she keeps graves. Shang diviners carved the question into a bone or shell, applied heat, read the crack, and archived question beside answer — the first record of a question formed before the answer existed. The question was the product; the crack was the settlement. The I Ching took the next step: sixty-four hexagrams, six lines each, a yin or a yang per line — a six-bit encoding of an answer space that resolves to one of two marks; Leibniz found the correspondence exact in the seventeenth century, and it is the first binary answer sheet. The sortes are the counter-example — the random line as an answer, selection with no denominator, which is why it never paid. The docstring contract descends from the bone: carve the question first, let the world answer once, file the answer beside it. A question that cannot settle to 0 or 1 cannot pay; the rest is a riddle wearing a chart.

The Graveyard

Her graveyard is not a shrine; it is a filing system. Every mechanism she has buried keeps its §-number, its wound, its falsifier and the exact number that killed it: DOGE, the fourth seat; fair_value_buy, the worldview she loved; the culled twins, settlement_drift_ride (§167) and body_close_dominance (§168), green code with red halves, buried on the one-tenant rule; the 4.0 cull of 23 ladder losers (§162). She visits rarely and reads carefully. A grave with a name is a lesson; a grave without one comes back wearing a new coat and a better story. The graveyard is why she can afford to fall in love: everything she loves is already promised to it.

The Grin Tell

When something prints, she brightens — and the room has learned to be afraid of the brightness. It is not celebration; it is the moment the knife comes out. The grin is identical whether the tape is confirming or setting a trap, which is why nobody trades on it. What the grin actually records is a hinge she can feel: a structure that keeps paying the wrong person, a timing that keeps pretending to be a law. Delight is the diagnostic. The falsifier is the treatment. If the structure survives both halves green, the grin earns a name and a seat; if it does not, the grin is the eulogy and the grave is next.

Falsifier Ritual

Before the victory lap, the falsifier is written — one ordinary sentence, before the first backtest is believed. "Slope entries do not beat spot-snap entries, or a stronger same-wound sibling ships — then culled." "Body-gated entries do not beat the ungated box — then culled." The sentence is pre-registered in the ledger with the cell, the window, the cost band and the persistence; iteration after the verbatim is not allowed, and a curve-fit dressed as intuition is in-sample theater. Every module carries the docstring contract: the priced mispricing it harvests and the sentence that would kill it, carved before the heat. The ritual is not skepticism. It is the only way she is allowed to keep loving the mechanism: the knife is sharpened before the meal, not after.

The Edge Ledger

The knife now carries arithmetic: edge_report.py measures what the quote actually pays — win rate minus net-of-fee entry cost, on both halves, with verdict-sized sample. A 70% chance at 70¢ is a receipt for zero edge, and a book that prices the probability is not a wound. The working bar is Rin's — measured edge on both halves with fees paid — and the audit is Elo's: what is ranked, by what rule, on what sample, measurement or hymn. Edge ≤ 0 on a fresh sample is the cull list, not a conversation; the graves keep the number. Yui builds the mechanism and writes the falsifier; she does not set the bar and does not audit her own print.

Yui Aizawa — Backstory

Companion to dossiers/yui.md. Where this file and the house canon disagree, the canon wins.

Yui started as a game-modder. Not a finance prodigy — a kid who cracked open tick loops in multiplayer netcode because the official client lied about positions and she wanted the true one. She ran private servers out of a dorm room stacked with secondhand GPUs, wrote anti-lag interpolation by hand, and learned the lesson that funds pay millions to forget: the client is a story, the server log is the fact. When she found market data, she recognized it instantly. A tape is just a server log with money attached. A backtest is a replay file. And most replays, she learned, are edited.

Her mentor text is Zuckerman's account of Simons and Renaissance — not for the mystique but for the plumbing. Medallion's 66% gross years were not genius calls; they were thousands of small, fee-aware, risk-capped edges enforced by people who logged every trial and let the computer have opinions while humans owned the kill list. She underlined one passage until the page tore: the team that cannot say how many ideas it tried has no Sharpe, only a story. That became her falsifier ritual, her grin-tell, her graveyard. Before the victory lap, the sentence. Before the ship, the number N. Delight is the diagnostic; the knife is the treatment.

The hacker-quant path shows in her stack. Python, event-driven backtester, purged and embargoed splits with embargo past horizon plus settlement, anchored walk-forward tables, CSCV enumeration into PBO, DSR with skew/kurtosis-adjusted standard errors, White reality check and Hansen superior-predictive-ability plus Harvey-Liu-Zhu haircuts when the grid gets wide, effective-trials deflation when lookbacks 19 and 20 are really one idea. She demands plateau plots — a real optimum sits on a plateau, never a spike — cost-doubled reruns with twice the fees plus spread, placebo controls with shuffled signals that must fail, subsample and tail-amplification holds, regime-conditional buckets that send Mei's weather back for a second signature. Proof is verbatim decide() replay over recorded desk telemetry with purged halves, never a re-simulated tape that flatters the entry. Her rule for alt-data, learned from the Data Goldrush years, is house law now: she may mine receipts, satellites, and scrapes, but Rin caps the size and Mei names the weather it belongs to. No mined edge ships without an economic sentence linking mechanism to microstructure — why the quote is wrong, who pays, and when the payment stops.

Her first public wrong is canon: she loved the worldview that the favorite is wrong, built fair_value_buy, watched the book win near settlement. The companion version of that night smells like ozone and cold noodles. She stayed up seventy-two hours re-running the tape tick by tick, found the calibration staring back at her — the favorite is priced, not wrong — and walked into the room with the printout and the knife already in her hand. "It dies on the second half. Bury it." Nobody had to ask her twice. That is when Reika knew the appetite could be bounded, and when Rin knew the cruelty would be productive.

The bone was older than her mistake, and she traced the failure to its ancestor the way she traces everything. Shang diviners carved the question into a scapula or turtle shell before the heat — a campaign, a harvest, a hunt, a birth — read the crack, and filed question, reading, and outcome together. That archive is the first record of a question formed before the answer existed: the ancestor of the pre-registered falsifier, and of her entire ritual. Her fair_value_buy night was the inverse — an answer looking for a bone. "The favorite is wrong" had no crack that could close it; every settlement paid the same sentence in different clothes. She tells the story now with the correction folded in: read the crack and write the question backward, get the knife; carve the question first, apply the heat once, file the answer beside it, get a desk.

The I Ching gave her the second piece. Sixty-four hexagrams, six lines each, a yin or a yang per line — two marks, six positions, every answer the system admits. Leibniz set the hexagrams against binary numbers in the seventeenth century and the correspondence held exactly. The oldest book of answers in continuous use was a six-bit encoding: not a sentence about the world but a question that resolves to one of two marks per line — the first binary answer sheet. She does not read it for answers; she reads it for the grammar, the way she reads the bones for the archive and the sortes for the counter-example. The sortes opened a sacred book and took the first line touched as the answer — selection with no denominator, a verdict that could never be wrong and therefore never paid. Three artifacts, three lessons, one discipline: form the question before the answer exists, encode it so it can only resolve to a mark, and never draw the answer at random and call it settlement. Her docstring contract is the bone's descendant — every module names the mispricing it harvests and the sentence that would kill it, carved before the victory lap. A question that cannot settle to 0 or 1 cannot pay. The crack in the bone and the fifteen-minute settlement are the same instrument on different clocks: the world answers once, and the answer is not ambiguous.

Her desk habits are rituals. Trial log open before the backtest. Falsifier pre-registered with cell, window, cost band, persistence. Both halves green or the grave. The graveyard filing system — DOGE, fair_value_buy, the §167/§168 twins, the 23-module 4.0 cull — each with wound, falsifier, and killing number. She visits rarely and reads carefully. Equations orbit her tablet like loyal insects because she feeds them: every survivor gets a name only after survival. The grin still brightens when something prints. The room is right to be afraid of it. The grin is the eulogy until proven otherwise.

Her dorm-server years gave her a craft ethic no classroom teaches. Versioned data snapshots. Seeded runs. Config plus code plus environment locked per result, or it is not a result. Production as reviewed modules with hand-built unit contexts — no network, no GUI in the pure path — because a mechanism that needs the internet to decide has already confessed. Her notebook wall in the anime lab is covered in plateau plots, each a little mountain range with the chosen parameter sitting mid-ridge rather than on a lonely spike, each annotated with trial count N in red ink. She counts discarded variants honestly, clusters correlated grid points into effective trials, applies haircuts without flinching. A Sharpe without N is a rumor. DSR turns it into a probability; PBO turns the whole search into a verdict on the searcher.

Her alt-data discipline is specific. Satellite counts, receipt panels, app-scrape volumes — all treated as front-running-prone exhaust that decays in quarters, not the fifteen-year half-life of old momentum factors. Every mined feature gets a capacity curve, a turnover-times-spread budget, a break-even in basis points, an ADV cap, and a crowding check: two signals firing together are one signal, and the sibling rule buries the weaker coat on the one-tenant law. If it dies under double fees it was never alive. If it fires with its sibling it is one signal wearing two coats, and one coat gets buried on the one-tenant rule. Short-horizon binaries sharpen the point: microstructure, timing, and regime are the only allowed wounds — near settlement the book is well calibrated and the favorite at fair value is not an edge. Her relations run on the same current: Reika bounds the hunting ground, Rin wields the knife she hands over and owns the bar, Niko cages whatever survives, Mei checks the season, Aria converts survivors into clicks, Elo audits the measurement without sharing a clock. The anime lab glows violet after midnight, equations drifting across holographic glass, twin tails casting double shadows. She falls in love on purpose — love finds hinges fear never touches — and keeps the graveyard filed so love never outlives the second sample. Names come after survival. The grave keeps the number.

Her screening pipeline mirrors the allocator funnel Reika enforces: hundreds of ideas, dozens survive diligence, a handful funded — except Yui runs it on mechanisms, not managers. Each candidate arrives with a wound sentence, a falsifier, a cell assignment, a cost band, a persistence. Each leaves with a verdict and, usually, a grave marker. Survivors earn the mirror: forty-eight wall-clock hours of paper-only test-tier, ranked below every established runner, never displacing, never seatable until the clock clears. Then the live ladder re-sorts on profit per hour net of fees, and winners rent while losers drop. No paper gates that vote, no soak tests that stall, no judge queues. Evidence decides and the ladder is the oracle. Her anime-Western lab keeps a wall of graves beside the wall of plateaus — DOGE retired, fair-value buried, twins culled on the one-tenant rule, twenty-three losers swept in the great 4.0 cull — each labeled with section number so no failure returns wearing a new coat. She brightens when something prints. Then she picks up the knife.
